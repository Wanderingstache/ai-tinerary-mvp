The Truth About Travel

How the industry changed—and who's really to blame

Travel agents used to be trusted advisors. They spent years learning, building expertise, and earning their clients' respect. But the industry didn't change because of them. It changed because of consolidation, predatory "democratization," and a system that squeezes legitimate professionals into becoming transactional middlemen. This is the real story.

The Short Version

Everybody Takes a Slice

Agents earn commission from the hotels, cruise lines and tours they book: about 10% for hotels, 10–18% for cruises. Most independent agents then hand 20–30% of it to a host agency, and the host usually belongs to a consortium with "preferred" suppliers. Thin margins reward volume, and whoever pays a little more.

Anyone Can Be an "Agent"

No federal license is required in the US, and only a handful of states require seller-of-travel registration. Pay-to-join travel MLMs live in that gap. Ask any agent who certified them, who hosts them, and how they get paid on your trip.

The €20 Million Receipt

A Full Experience Colosseum ticket is €24 on the official site. Resold as a private tour it runs about $540–670 a person. In April 2025, Italy's competition authority fined the Colosseum's ticket seller and six resellers almost €20 million for bots hoarding tickets to resell in pricier bundles.

Algorithms Don't Know You

The big booking sites win on scale. They'll find you a cheap room. They won't tell you the street is loud or that every restaurant worth eating at is across town.
